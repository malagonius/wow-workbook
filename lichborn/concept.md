# Lichborn — Concept

> **The longer the fight goes, the more inevitable your victory feels.**

## Core fantasy

The spec is about **dominating the enemy**.

The desired player fantasy is to feel like **Arthas / the next Lich King**: a slow, methodical juggernaut whose opponent becomes progressively less able to resist Death's will.

Core emotional premise:

> **Kill it before it kills you, because it will.**

The ultimate fantasy is not simply killing an enemy. It is making the enemy's defeat feel predetermined because the Lich has established an irreversible claim over it.

## The central loop

1. Establish **Death's Presence** on an enemy.
2. Dominion accumulates over time.
3. The enemy progressively loses agency, not merely health.
4. The Lich chooses how that accumulated dominion is expressed.
5. At extreme dominion, the enemy is effectively **100% dominated**.
6. When the enemy dies, its death may produce a consequence based on how much dominion was established and/or how that dominion was expressed.

## Dominion

The current conceptual model is a universal **Dominion** state rather than several unrelated diseases.

Dominion should feel like one underlying supernatural claim:

> **You don't have different diseases. You have one disease: Death's claim over you.**

Possible expressions include:

- **Death** — damage and eventual killing pressure.
- **Submission** — denial of actions or abilities.
- **Servitude** — temporary control where balance permits.
- **Reanimation** — what the enemy's death becomes.

These are concepts, not yet final mechanics.

## Inevitability

Dominion should not naturally fall off on a normal short timer.

A major counterplay vector is therefore **distance / escaping the Lich**, rather than simply waiting for a debuff to expire.

The longer the Lich remains engaged with the target, the more dangerous the situation becomes.

## Enemy agency

A defining difference from a normal damage-over-time spec is that Dominion should eventually affect what the enemy **can do**.

Possible examples:

- Preventing a Kick.
- Preventing a Slow/Freedom-like effect.
- Preventing interactions with mechanics.
- Suppressing effects analogous to Grounding Totem interactions.
- Other expressions where the Lich visibly imposes its will.

The presentation should feel like **domination**, not generic crowd control.

## Death as consequence

The enemy's corpse is not the main objective, but it can be a cool consequence of successful domination.

A working idea is that the resulting temporary servant/pet depends on the amount or type of dominion established before death.

> **The way you dominated this creature determines what its death becomes.**

The more effort/time spent establishing dominion, the stronger the resulting consequence should be.

Pets and an undead army are therefore **by-products**, not the core gameplay objective. This is important because Unholy already owns the fantasy of being the Death Knight with an undead army.

## Identity vs existing Death Knight specs

- **Blood:** You cannot kill me.
- **Frost:** You cannot withstand me.
- **Unholy:** I bring death, decay, and undead.
- **Lichborn:** **You cannot escape what your death has already become.**

Another useful distinction:

- Unholy: **I have an army.**
- Lichborn: **You are supplying my army / your death belongs to me.**

## Lore / fantasy boundaries

Powerful enemies may potentially be claimed if the gameplay can support it. Balance takes priority over literal fantasy: the concept should not require an unbalanceable permanent mind-control mechanic.
